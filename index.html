CVR-GS: Illumination-Robust Novel View Synthesis via Cross-View Relighting

Anonymous Author(s)
Paper arXiv Code

Abstract

While neural rendering methods such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excel at novel view synthesis (NVS) under photometrically consistent lighting, they often fail in complex real-world environments. In unconstrained scenarios, varying illumination severely corrupts cross-view photometric consistency. Globally, entire images suffer from unpredictable under- or over-exposure. Locally, critical visual information is destroyed as highlights saturate and shadows collapse into noise.

To address this, we recast illumination-robust novel view synthesis as a cross-view relighting problem and propose CVR-GS. We synthesize one view under another view's illumination condition and constrain the reconstruction with illumination alignment and structural consistency, yielding a shared, illumination-invariant canonical appearance representation from complex per-view lighting. Furthermore, a per-Gaussian reliability score derived from the deviation between local and global illumination stabilizes densification and pruning in unreliable regions, such as shadows or saturated highlights, preventing geometry corruption.

CVR-GS achieves higher-quality NVS than prior methods across diverse benchmarks, from multi-exposure to extreme lighting conditions.

Method Overview

Overview of CVR-GS pipeline. CVR-GS pairs illumination-complementary views and extracts cross-view features to decompose each Gaussian's color into canonical base appearance $B$, detail refinement $\Phi$, ambient illumination $E_{\text{env}}$, and per-view local shading $E_{\text{loc}}$. Cross-view relighting enforces consistency by exchanging illumination between paired views, while illumination-reliability guided optimization suppresses densification and pruning in degraded regions.

We categorize each training view's exposure into three levels: O (overexposed), M (mid exposure), and U (underexposed). We evaluate under four exposure distribution protocols, each defining the proportion of O/M/U views in the training set: (i) Bright-skewed (80% O / 10% M / 10% U), (ii) Dark-skewed (80% U / 10% M / 10% O), (iii) Uniform Random (equal ~33% O / ~33% M / ~33% U mix), and (iv) Extreme Bimodal (50% O / 50% U, no mid-exposure). All use a fixed random seed for reproducibility.
