Extreme-GS: Illumination-Consistent 3D Scene Representation under Extreme Lighting

Anonymous Author(s)*
*Anonymous Institution
AAAI 2027
Paper arXiv Code

Extreme-GS recovers illumination-consistent 3D scene representations under extreme lighting conditions including mixed exposure, pure low-light, pure over-exposure, and synthetic extreme lighting.

Abstract

Current novel view synthesis (NVS) methods, notably Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), fundamentally rely on multi-view photometric consistency. However, this assumption is frequently violated in uncontrolled real-world environments.

Under extreme lighting, images suffer from both global exposure shifts and local illumination artifacts—such as severe over/under-exposure, saturated highlights, and cast shadows. These view-dependent degradations destroy appearance consistency and introduce unreliable optimization signals, making it exceedingly difficult to recover illumination-consistent 3D scene representations.

To address this challenge, we propose Extreme-GS, a self-supervised structured 3D Gaussian framework for modeling scenes under extreme lighting. Extreme-GS introduces a structured shading representation that implicitly separates a stable scene component from lighting-dependent modulation. Dual-view feature-driven relighting constrains this ambiguous factorization by transferring illumination across views while preserving scene structure. We further estimate illumination reliability to modulate geometry optimization, suppressing unreliable densification and pruning in saturated, shadowed, or locally corrupted regions.

Experiments on mixed-exposure, pure low-light, pure over-exposure, and synthetic extreme-lighting settings show that Extreme-GS yields more consistent scene appearance and improves view synthesis quality across challenging extreme-lighting datasets.

Method Overview

Overview of Extreme-GS. Given multi-view images with extreme lighting variations, Extreme-GS decomposes scene appearance into stable content and lighting-dependent modulation via a structured shading representation. A cross-view feature extraction network enables dual-view relighting for self-supervision, while illumination-reliability guided optimization (IRGO) suppresses unreliable geometry updates in corrupted regions.

Method

Structured Shading Model

We introduce a multiplicative decomposition of appearance into a stable scene component and lighting-dependent modulation. Each Gaussian stores a base color B, a shading embedding Φ, and environment/local illumination codes Eenv and Eloc. The final rendered color is:

C = B · (1 + Φ) · (Eenv + Eloc)

This formulation enables the model to disentangle view-consistent scene content from view-dependent illumination effects, enabling robust novel view synthesis under arbitrary lighting conditions.

Cross-View Relighting

To constrain the inherently ambiguous shading decomposition, we introduce dual-view relighting as self-supervision. Geometry- and illumination-aware view selection identifies informative peer views. Cross-view attention extracts corresponding features, enabling illumination transfer while preserving structural consistency.

Two complementary losses guide the optimization:

  • Illumination alignment loss Lillum — aligns illumination between rendered and relit views.
  • Structure consistency loss Lstruct — preserves geometric and textural details during relighting.

Illumination-Reliability Guided Optimization (IRGO)

Saturated, shadowed, or corrupted regions produce unreliable gradients that mislead 3DGS density estimation. IRGO estimates a per-Gaussian confidence score based on chromatic alignment and local appearance consistency, then modulates:

  • Densification: Gaussians in low-confidence regions are penalized, preventing overfitting to corrupted measurements.
  • Pruning: Unreliable Gaussians are pruned more aggressively, maintaining a clean and compact scene representation.

Combined with progressive log-space optimization for handling wide dynamic range, IRGO enables stable training and high-quality rendering across diverse extreme-lighting inputs.

Experiments

Datasets

We evaluate on four challenging extreme-lighting benchmarks:

Multi-exposure

Real scenes with bracketed exposures (±3EV) from GaussHDR / HDR-NeRF, covering diverse indoor and outdoor environments.

Synthetic

8 NeRF Blender scenes re-rendered with dynamic point lights and weak ambient illumination, providing ground truth for evaluation.

DarkGS

Real low-light scenes with localized flashlight illumination, testing robustness to severe underexposure.

LOM

Pure low-light and pure over-exposure settings with normal-exposure ground truth for reference-based evaluation.

Multi-Exposure (HDR) Results

Qualitative comparison on multi-exposure datasets. Extreme-GS produces more consistent scene appearance with fewer artifacts in saturated and shadowed regions compared to baselines.

Extreme Multi-Exposure Results

Results on extreme multi-exposure scenes. Our method maintains consistent appearance across large exposure variations where baseline methods struggle.

Low-Light Results

Qualitative comparison on DarkGS low-light dataset. Extreme-GS effectively recovers scene details from severely underexposed inputs.

Low- and Over-Exposure (LOM) Results

Results on LOM dataset. Our method handles both pure low-light and pure over-exposure scenarios with consistently high quality.

Synthetic Extreme-Lighting Results

Results on synthetic extreme-lighting dataset. Extreme-GS accurately reconstructs scene appearance under controlled ground-truth evaluation.

Structure Preservation Analysis

Structure preservation visualization. The structured shading representation and structure consistency loss effectively preserve geometric details during relighting.

Relighting Results

Dual-view relighting results. Our cross-view relighting framework enables illumination transfer across views while preserving scene structure.

Ablation Study

Ablation experiments. Each component—structured shading, cross-view relighting, and illumination-reliability guidance—contributes meaningfully to the final performance.
