CVR-GS: Illumination-Robust Novel View Synthesis via Cross-View Relighting

ShuangYu Feng1, Xiangbo Wang1, Xianghui Fan1, Xiangru Huang1
1 Westlake University
Paper arXiv Code

Abstract

Scene digitization and free-viewpoint visualization require detailed 3D reconstructions with consistent appearance. However, exposure changes and spatially varying illumination in practical captures can obscure scene details and disrupt cross-view photometric consistency, making reliable reconstruction challenging. Yet details obscured by underexposure, saturation, or local shadows in one view may remain visible in another. To exploit these complementary observations, we propose CVR-GS, a 3D Gaussian Splatting framework based on cross-view relighting. By exchanging illumination between views, CVR-GS combines illumination alignment and multi-scale structural consistency to constrain appearance–illumination decomposition and recover shared appearance for novel-view rendering. We further use illumination reliability to guide Gaussian densification and pruning, mitigating erroneous growth and premature pruning caused by unreliable photometric gradients. When complementary observations are insufficient, a small set of enhanced anchor views guides recovery in low-light and overexposed scenes. Experiments across multi-exposure, Multi-Light Objects, flashlight, and one-sided exposure settings demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in cross-view fusion and appearance recovery.

Method Overview

Overview of CVR-GS pipeline. CVR-GS pairs illumination-complementary views and learns an appearance–illumination decomposition of Gaussian colors for consistent appearance recovery. Cross-view relighting enforces consistency by exchanging illumination between paired views, while illumination reliability guides Gaussian densification and pruning.
