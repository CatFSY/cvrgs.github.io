CVR-GS: Illumination-Robust Novel View Synthesis via Cross-View Relighting

Anonymous Author(s)
Paper arXiv Code

Abstract

Novel view synthesis (NVS) with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assumes photometric consistency across views, yet real captures routinely violate it. Illumination corrupts each view both globally, through whole-image under- or over-exposure, and locally, where highlights saturate and shadows collapse into noise, destroying information within each view.

To integrate complementary information across these imperfect views, we recast illumination-robust scene recovery as a cross-view relighting problem and propose CVR-GS. Our method uses a structured shading representation to separate the underlying scene appearance from per-view illumination. By relighting one view with another's illumination and enforcing consistency, we disentangle this lighting from a shared, illumination-invariant canonical scene. From this separation we also obtain a per-Gaussian reliability score that stabilizes densification and pruning in saturated or shadowed regions, where photometric gradients are otherwise unreliable.

CVR-GS requires no ground-truth normal-light reference, exposure labels, or BRDF decomposition, and spans a spectrum from mixed-exposure captures to one-sided, fully extreme lighting. Across diverse extreme-lighting benchmarks, CVR-GS recovers a consistent scene and achieves higher-quality NVS than prior state-of-the-art methods.

Method Overview

CVR-GS takes multi-view images under extreme lighting and decomposes scene appearance into stable content and lighting-dependent modulation via a structured shading representation. Cross-view feature extraction enables dual-view relighting for self-supervision, while illumination-reliability guided optimization (IRGO) suppresses unreliable geometry updates in corrupted regions.

We categorize each training view's exposure into three levels: O (overexposed), M (mid exposure), and U (underexposed). We evaluate under four exposure distribution protocols, each defining the proportion of O/M/U views in the training set: (i) Bright-skewed (80% O / 10% M / 10% U), (ii) Dark-skewed (80% U / 10% M / 10% O), (iii) Uniform Random (equal ~33% O / ~33% M / ~33% U mix), and (iv) Extreme Bimodal (50% O / 50% U, no mid-exposure). All use a fixed random seed for reproducibility.
